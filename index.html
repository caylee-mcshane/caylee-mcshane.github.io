Research

In progress · 2026

Fragmented Oversight: How Multi-Agent Team Structure Affects Monitorability Under Distributed Attacks

An AI-control study of what happens when several agents built on the same model coordinate toward a hidden objective. Prior work shows per-agent monitoring fragments when several agents split an attack between them, and sets aside the monitor that sees the whole attack as undeployable because a defender cannot tell which actions belong together; in an orchestrated team, the orchestrator layer can tell, and this project measures whether monitoring there restores oversight.

Building

Co-founder & lead developer

An AI study platform that adapts to how students learn

I built the product end to end. It's designed as a guidance system rather than a content generator: it models what a student has and hasn't mastered from their own course material and produces targeted practice from it, so the value lives in the feedback loop, not the generation. Shipping it in production is where I sharpened how I think about AI tools — especially the quiet failure modes, where a model's output looks right but corrupts the signal underneath. It's the same instinct that pulls me toward safety.

Co-founder & lead developer

Control software for autonomous laboratory robotics

I'm the lead developer of the system that plans experiments and drives lab instruments and robotics. Its architecture keeps a deterministic safety layer independent of the AI: no model output reaches a physical action without passing validators that enforce the system's hard limits. Designing that boundary — capable models proposing, deterministic checks disposing — is a control problem, and it's part of what drew me to safety research.

I'm the lead developer on both. I also build and orchestrate my own teams of AI agents to develop and review the code — the same hands-on multi-agent engineering that grounds my research.
